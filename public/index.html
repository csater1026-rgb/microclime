How Microclime works

Weather apps report your whole zip code. Microclime figures out what the sun and cold actually do at one exact spot in your yard — using real astronomy and a photo you trace yourself, not a guess. Here's what each part does, in order:

1
Where is this spot?
Set the exact location (or use your device's location) and a date. This is what the sun-position math is calculated against.
2
Trace your real horizon
Take or upload a photo from that exact spot and trace the skyline — where trees, roofs, or fences actually block the sky. This is what makes the sun-hours specific to your spot instead of a generic estimate.
3
Your spot's real sun-hours
Combines the sun's real position with your traced horizon to show, hour by hour, whether that spot is in direct sun or shade — plus live frost and heat-stress risk, adjusted for this exact spot instead of the general forecast.
4
Calibrate to your spot
Log what actually happened overnight (the real low, whether it frosted) and the model learns a correction for your exact spot — it gets more accurate the more you use it.
5
What if you made a change?
Save your horizon as "how it is now," then edit the trace to test an idea — like planting or removing a tree — and see exactly how it would change your sun-hours and frost risk before you touch a shovel.
6
Your yard's sun zones
Save more than one spot and they're automatically grouped into Full sun, Partial sun, or Mostly shade — each with a real planting suggestion, so you know what to grow where.
7
Your block's microclimate map
A preview of what a shared neighborhood map would look like if people nearby used Microclime too — shown with demo data, since it needs more than one household to be real.

No accounts, no login — everything you save lives only in this browser.

Take a photo from the exact spot, holding your phone level and pointed at one direction. Then trace the skyline — where trees, roofs, or fences block the sky. Click along the photo to mark the blocking height at each point.

In degrees, like a compass: 0 = North, 90 = East, 180 = South, 270 = West. Open the Compass app on your phone, face the same way you took the photo, and type the number it shows.
